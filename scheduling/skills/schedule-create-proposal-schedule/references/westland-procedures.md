## Introduction & Overview

This document defines the process for developing, maintaining, and implementing a detailed Project CPM Schedule. The project schedule will undergo several stages of development, complexity, and detail during the preconstruction, construction, and closeout phases of the project. Westland is working to establish a more systematic approach to scheduling. Rather than start from scratch with each new project, we will create detailed accurate baseline template schedules for each standard project and building type. From there, schedules will be carefully adjusted and customized to fit the specific project. **This should never be a boiler plate process.** The activities, logic, and durations for each schedule must be unique. However, the basic structure, organization, and workflow should be consistent across all Westland projects.

We must be disciplined in our approach to scheduling. This will require consistent effort up front to ensure we have truly digested the documents and understand the project. We must consult with the people who will actually do the work and ensure we have accurate sequencing, good durations, realistic time frames for shop drawings, review, fabrication, and shipping. It is typically necessary to do some guess work to complete an initial schedule. However, **teams must resist the temptation to then assume the schedule is accurate.** It may be a great outline, but without real input from trade partners, suppliers, design teams, owners, etc. the schedule will not be a true reflection of how the work will progress. Westland will also work to implement additional tools to facilitate schedule health and risk analyses enabling each project team to create an accurate, achievable, and measurable roadmap to project success.

Please utilize the following outline as a reference at each stage of the scheduling process.

This document will also eventually outline how to utilize the project schedule to properly develop and populate the Supply Chain Log and its interface with the Domo Dashboard system.

## Schedule Development

### Proposal Schedule Request
- Business Development / Directors request a proposal schedule from Scheduling Department
  - This request should be submitted to the Scheduling Department Manager as early as possible, but **no less than 14 days** before the schedule is needed.

### Develop Conceptual Project Schedule with Project Proposal
- Utilize a Westland template, or ensure organization and formatting adhere to Westland's standards. (See guidelines for organization, WBS elements, and formatting below.)
- Obtain a detailed design & permitting schedule from the design team. Guide the design team to include sufficient information to accurately track progress.
- Include the design process in Westland's conceptual schedule.
- Coordinate with estimating / preconstruction to provide appropriate detail and durations for preconstruction deliverables, prequalification, buyout, etc.
- Include a procurement section for key materials, and identify any materials needing early release, expedited reviews, or careful monitoring due to market issues. Include all long lead items and materials identified as critical or likely to experience delays during the submittal or fabrication process. **Include fabrication and shipping activities and secure valid durations.**
- Develop a clear **Site Logistics & Workflow Plan** prior to creating the detailed schedule. (See `site-logistics-examples/` for reference images.)
- Provide a detailed construction schedule with typical activities and durations for the proposed building.
- Ensure the level of detail corresponds to the amount of design information available.
  - Consult with team members
  - Reference similar projects and construction types
  - Use reasonable assumptions and historical data to compensate for incomplete design
  - Include Westland standard responsibility codes and assign each task a responsible party

### Update Activities & Logic at Each Phase
- **Design** — As required by design progress and preconstruction agreement.
- **Preconstruction** — As required by the preconstruction agreement.
- **Construction** — Weekly.
- **Closeout** — Produce a final record schedule documenting the completion of all contract work, closeout items, and applicable owner occupancy activities.

### Fully Develop Detailed Baseline Construction Schedule
- Meet with the project director and PMT to develop a graphic schedule detailing the anticipated scope, identifying general areas, zoning, phasing, and flow for all site development and structures included in the project. **The Superintendent must be an integral part of this process.**
- The project scheduler then creates the tasks and logic in the CPM software and issues a draft for PMT review.
- PMT provides an in-depth review and feedback on the draft schedule.
- PMT and project scheduler then work with trade partners and suppliers to perform any needed pull planning to confirm scope, establish durations, and secure buy-in. A digital process is acceptable, provided key team members are directly involved in the process or are performing concurrent reviews and providing active feedback.
- Run reports utilizing the SmartPM Quality Checker and the P6 Check Schedule Report to ensure all schedule activities have sound logic, optimal sequencing, and appropriate predecessor and successor relationships. **This step is critical prior to establishing the baseline version of the schedule.**

### Establish Baseline Schedule
- Establish Baseline Schedule and submit for approval to Owner. The Baseline Schedule will be finalized and set once the Notice to Proceed is issued by the Owner. Typically required within 10 days of contract execution.
- In **Primavera P6 Professional**, once accepted, assign the approved version as the Baseline Schedule and export an XER file as a record prior to commencing any updates.
- In **Phoenix Project Manager**, save a separate copy of the schedule as the Baseline Version and establish the primary Storepoint before commencing any updates.
- Create a new project in **SmartPM** and upload the baseline schedule to commence schedule performance tracking and provide access to schedule analytics. In the rare case of a project being too short to warrant a SmartPM project, the Quality Checker must be utilized to ensure compliance with best practices and standards.

## Schedule Maintenance & Updates

- Ongoing schedule maintenance and updates are required at all stages of the project. Fewer updates will be required during preconstruction, but design progress must be accurately tracked.
- Utilize the preconstruction period to carefully review logic, sequencing, durations, relationships, etc. and make necessary adjustments and corrections.

### Update Accepted Preconstruction Proposal Schedule

**Preconstruction Schedule Updates:**
- Schematic Design
- Design Development
- 70% Construction Documents
- 100% Construction Documents and/or
- As needed / required during Design

**Preconstruction Schedule Deliverables:**
- Updated Design Schedule
- Extract required Rolling Schedules directly from the updated Baseline Schedule during Preconstruction Services. This schedule will be attached to the Preconstruction Reports submitted to the Owner as required by the preconstruction agreement.
- Any required filters:
  - Trades
  - Critical Path
  - Specific Section of Schedule, etc.

### Construction Phase Updates

- The Scheduling Manager and Lead Scheduler will determine who will perform schedule updates and maintenance during construction. As we train more Project Engineers and Superintendents we will be able to delegate these functions more frequently. But the Scheduling Department will maintain oversight and assist with any changes to logic, sequencing, durations, etc. until the project is complete.
- Schedules shall be updated **Weekly**, or Bi-Monthly if warranted, but no less than every other week.
- The full Westland project team should participate in these updates. Updates may also be performed with the trades during the weekly coordination meeting. This is ideal, but may not be feasible on international projects.
- Before commencing an update, create a new dated schedule update folder on the shared drive, or wherever the project team maintains project files. Preserve a record of the current version of the schedule:
  - **Phoenix:** prior to commencing the update, create a copy of the last updated file and rename it to the current date. The previous update remains unchanged and is the record file.
  - **P6:** once the update is complete, export an XER file while the project schedule is still open. Place this record version in the same folder as the current update and PDF reports.
- At all stages of the project, clearly document changes made to the schedule including altering relationships, adding activities, deleting activities, splitting activities, etc. **This is done automatically when a project update is uploaded to SmartPM.**
- Store a copy of the current updated schedule in the appropriate schedule folder on the G:\ Drive, Procore, or other project file location. Store another copy on the hard drive of your machine — this allows schedule work to continue if the network or server is down.
- Issue a Weekly Update Report via email and copy the full PMT, Project Director, Estimator, and the Scheduling Team. Include all elements outlined in the Schedule Update Email Procedure guidelines.

**Construction Schedule Deliverables at each update:**
- Updated Baseline Schedule
- Critical Path Schedule
- Longest Path Schedule
- 4-Week Rolling Schedule — extracted from the updated Baseline Schedule. Used to assist Superintendents in developing their more detailed 4-Week schedules and for coordination with trade contractors.
- Any required or beneficial filters:
  - Trades – Responsible Parties
  - Procurement
  - Other Specific Sections of Schedule, etc.
  - Any reports or filters required by the project specifications
- Upload the current update through Procore Drive to enable the use of the Procore scheduling tool.
- Assist PMTs with the creation and maintenance of graphic schedules.

## Managing & Documenting Schedule Impacts

- Any issue impacting, or potentially impacting, the project schedule must be properly documented.
- The scheduler should insert impact activities that tie into the existing schedule logic to clearly demonstrate the effect the issue is having on the completion of current and subsequent activities, as well as any impact to project completion.
- Anticipated delays should be published and presented to the owner's representative and project team as soon as Westland's team is aware of an impact, or potential impact. **Clear, honest, timely communication is vital** to secure approval of any claims for time and added compensation.

### Time Impact Analysis (TIA)

Prior to submitting a claim, any delays on the project should be entered as IMPACT Activities into the schedule to communicate the delay. If it is determined the IMPACT activities are driving the Critical Path, a TIA must be developed. This is done by:

1. Creating a **Fragnet** (fragment of the schedule logic network) within the updated baseline schedule, tied to the activity the IMPACT is driving.
2. Creating an appropriate successor relationship with the next activity to show the effect on the Critical Path.
3. Once the schedule is updated and the fragnet prepared, use the standardized **Impact Analysis Form** to develop the TIA.

### Extension of Time (EOT) Submissions

- If at all possible, EOTs should be submitted in the form of a PCO on a monthly basis.
- Clearly indicate verbally and in writing when a delay is ongoing so the owner will know we will continue to submit future claims until the core issue is fully resolved.
- **If a delay is our fault** (caused by a trade contractor), we must do all in our power to create a solid recovery plan and avoid the delay. Rather than respond to trade delays or other issues by simply surrendering float or giving up critical path days and extending the schedule, we need to work as a team to secure buy-in and then hold our partners accountable to meet the recovery dates.

## Schedule Organization

Adhere as closely as possible to the following Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) sections and general organization of the schedule. Refer to the sample collapsed schedule demonstrating how this WBS should be applied to a real project. For adaptations of this structure (greenfield / occupied rebuild / phased turnover), see `wbs-patterns.md`.

### General Schedule Organization

```
PROJECT
  SUMMARY & MILESTONES
    CONTRACT MILESTONES & SUMMARY BARS
    KEY PERFORMANCE MILESTONES

  PRE-CONSTRUCTION
    DESIGN
      ESTIMATES - CONSTRUCTABILITY REPORTS - SCHEDULE UPDATES
      BIM MODELING
      TRADE PRE-QUALIFICATION
      BUY OUT - PROPOSAL & AWARD
      OWNER PERMIT / CONSENT PROCESS

  PROCUREMENT
    SUBMITTALS - APPROVALS - FABRICATION - DELIVERY

  CONSTRUCTION
    SITEWORK
      INITIAL SITEWORK
        CLEAR & GRUB - CUT & FILL - ROUGH GRADE - UTILITIES
      BALANCE OF SITEWORK
        HARDSCAPE - LANDSCAPE - EQUIPMENT ENCLOSURES
    STRUCTURE & SUBROUGH
      AREA - LEVEL
    BUILDING ENCLOSURE - WINDOWS - ENTRIES - FINISH SYSTEMS
      ELEVATION - AREA - LEVEL
    INTERIOR ROUGH-IN & FINISHES
      AREA - LEVEL

  COMMISSIONING & CLOSE-OUT  (may be shown by building, area, or floor)
```

### Minimum Standard Elements

Schedules shall contain the following elements as a minimum standard:

- **Milestones — one every 30 days** (critical path or significant event)
  - Include Westland standard required milestones and any others required by the project specifications.
- Prequalification Process / Mock-Ups
- Design (if CM/GC or Design-Build)
  - Schematic Design
  - Design Development
  - 70% Construction Documents
  - 100% Construction Documents
- Submittals / Procurement / Mock-Ups
- Preconstruction Activities
- Construction
  - Sitework
  - Structure & Sub-Rough
  - Building Enclosure
  - Interior Rough-In & Finishes
- Closeout
- **Progress Impacts** (construction-phase tracking only — not included in proposal schedules):
  - Weather Days
  - PR's / ASI's
  - Owner Delays
  - Contractor Delays
